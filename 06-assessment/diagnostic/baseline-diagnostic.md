# Grade VIII Baseline Diagnostic — Semester 1

## Status

`READY TO ADMINISTER — evidence not yet collected`

## Purpose

Diagnostic ini bukan ujian nilai dan bukan tes seluruh materi kelas VIII. Tujuannya memperoleh evidence minimum untuk gate `G0–G6` pada `04-curriculum-map/prerequisite-map.md`, sehingga starting point pembelajaran dapat dipilih sebagai `FOUNDATION`, `BRIDGE`, atau `TEXTBOOK-ALIGNED`.

## Administration Contract

- Intrakurikuler: **1 JP / 40 menit**.
- Jangan mengajarkan jawaban sebelum bagian terkait selesai.
- Instruksi tugas boleh dijelaskan singkat dalam Bahasa Indonesia agar yang diukur bukan kemampuan menebak instruksi tes.
- Untuk G0, gunakan observasi classroom language secara natural selama pelaksanaan.
- Tidak perlu mengejar semua siswa menghasilkan jawaban sempurna. Yang dicari adalah pola akses kelas.
- Diagnostic tidak digunakan untuk ranking siswa.

## 40-Minute Flow

| Time | Activity | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Classroom-language observation | G0 |
| 5–10 min | Familiar vocabulary | G1 |
| 10–17 min | Simple sentence access | G2 |
| 17–23 min | Positive / negative / question | G3 |
| 23–29 min | Very short reading | G4 |
| 29–34 min | Basic past-event readiness | G5 |
| 34–40 min | 2–3 connected ideas/events | G6 |

# A. Teacher Observation — G0 Classroom Access

Gunakan beberapa instruksi natural selama diagnostic, misalnya:

- `Open your book/notebook.`
- `Write your name.`
- `Work by yourself.`
- `Listen.`
- `Read the text.`
- `Stop writing.`

Amati juga apakah siswa dapat merespons atau menggunakan bentuk klarifikasi seperti:

- `Please repeat.`
- `What does ... mean?`
- `How do you say ... in English?`

Jangan mewajibkan kalimat klarifikasi persis sama. Bahasa Indonesia/local language untuk meminta bantuan tetap dicatat sebagai **strategic access**, bukan kegagalan total.

### G0 Evidence

- `INDEPENDENT`: mayoritas instruksi dasar dipahami tanpa terjemahan.
- `SUPPORTED`: instruksi dipahami setelah gesture/repetition/terjemahan singkat.
- `NOT YET`: instruksi dasar tetap sulit diikuti walaupun diberi dukungan sederhana.

# B. Student Sheet

## Part 1 — Words Around You (G1) — 5 min

### B1. Match

Pasangkan kata dengan arti yang tepat.

1. teacher  
2. tired  
3. eat  
4. yesterday  
5. school

A. kemarin  
B. makan  
C. sekolah  
D. guru  
E. lelah

### B2. Use

Lengkapi dengan satu kata yang paling cocok dari kotak.

`hungry — study — morning`

1. I am ________. I want to eat.  
2. We ________ English at school.  
3. I go to school in the ________.

**Evidence target:** familiar nouns, condition/feeling, common action, time word; recognition plus minimal contextual use.

## Part 2 — Very Simple Sentences (G2) — 7 min

### C1. Choose

1. I ___ a student.  
   a. am  b. is  c. are

2. She ___ tired.  
   a. am  b. is  c. are

3. They ___ at school.  
   a. am  b. is  c. are

4. We ___ English every Monday.  
   a. study  b. are  c. is

5. I ___ rice every morning.  
   a. eat  b. am  c. is

### C2. Complete the sentence

Lengkapi menjadi kalimat yang benar.

1. `He ______ a teacher.`
2. `They ______ football.`

**Evidence target:** usable distinction between `be` predicate and lexical/action verb; not mastery of terminology. The previous word-string form (`he — teacher` / `they — play — football`) was removed because the second item could be answered merely by deleting separators and did not provide a balanced diagnostic demand.

## Part 3 — Positive, Negative, Question (G3) — 6 min

Ubah sesuai perintah.

1. Positive: `She is tired.`  
   Negative: ________________________________

2. Positive: `They are students.`  
   Question: ________________________________ ?

3. Positive: `You play football.`  
   Negative: ________________________________

4. Positive: `You study English.`  
   Question: ________________________________ ?

### Optional oral probe if time permits

Teacher: `Are you tired?`  
Student: __________________

Teacher: `Do you like English?`  
Student: __________________

**Evidence target:** contrast among statement/negation/question, including both `be` and lexical verb. Do not require perfect spelling/punctuation for gate judgment.

---

## Part 4 — Read and Find Information (G4) — 6 min

Read:

> Rina is a student. She lives near her school. Every morning, she goes to school with Siti. Rina likes English, but she does not like math. After school, she plays badminton.

Answer briefly.

1. Who is Rina? ________________________________  
2. Who goes to school with Rina? __________________  
3. What subject does Rina like? ____________________  
4. What does she do after school? __________________

**Evidence target:** locate explicit information in a very short familiar written text. This task does not claim to measure inference or full Phase-D reading target.

---

## Part 5 — Yesterday (G5) — 5 min

### E1. Choose

1. Yesterday, I ___ at home.  
   a. am  b. was  c. are

2. Yesterday, they ___ tired.  
   a. were  b. are  c. is

3. Yesterday, I ___ football.  
   a. played  b. play  c. playing

4. Yesterday, she ___ to school.  
   a. went  b. go  c. goes

### E2. One past sentence

What did you do yesterday? Write **one simple sentence**. Bahasa Indonesia may be added below it if needed.

Yesterday, I ______________________________________.

**Evidence target:** recognize past-time reference, `was/were`, one regular and one high-frequency irregular past form, plus attempt one personally meaningful past-event sentence.

---

## Part 6 — Connect Ideas (G6) — 6 min

Write **2–3 short sentences** about yesterday. You may use the words below.

`yesterday — and — then — after that`

Prompts if needed:

- Where were you?
- What did you do?
- What did you do next?

__________________________________________________  
__________________________________________________  
__________________________________________________

If writing blocks the student completely, teacher may collect an oral attempt after the written diagnostic using the same prompts. Record the mode (`WRITTEN` / `ORAL`).

**Evidence target:** ability to connect 2–3 ideas/events with support. Grammar errors do not automatically fail the gate when meaning and event connection are clear.

# C. Gate Scoring

This diagnostic uses **gate evidence**, not a conventional 0–100 grade.

For G1–G6 assign one of:

- `INDEPENDENT` — target is demonstrated with little/no support.
- `SUPPORTED` — target is accessible with model, prompt, choice, repetition, or limited scaffolding.
- `NOT YET` — evidence is insufficient even with the built-in support.

Use item accuracy as evidence, not as a rigid mastery cutoff. Suggested interpretation anchors:

| Gate | Independent evidence | Supported evidence | Not Yet evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | most recognition + contextual items usable | recognition stronger than use | familiar vocabulary blocks task meaning |
| G2 | both `be` and action-verb patterns mostly usable | one pattern stable or production needs model | sentence meaning/pattern remains unclear |
| G3 | statement/negative/question contrast usable across both patterns | contrast partly usable, especially with `be` | transformation intent/structure largely inaccessible |
| G4 | explicit details located accurately | details found with rereading/teacher pointing to text | cannot reliably locate basic explicit details |
| G5 | past reference + several basic past forms recognized/attempted | recognizes time/past with choices but production weak | present/past contrast not yet accessible |
| G6 | 2–3 meaningfully connected ideas/events | connection possible with prompts/model or orally | isolated words/sentence only; sequence not yet accessible |

## Class-Level Decision Rule

Do **not** average all gates into one number. Record distribution per gate.

For planning, use this heuristic:

- If a gate is `NOT YET` for a substantial part of the class, that dependency enters the early `FOUNDATION` pathway.
- If mostly `SUPPORTED`, design a `BRIDGE` pathway with scaffolding and gradual removal of support.
- If mostly `INDEPENDENT`, do not spend weeks reteaching it; integrate it into `TEXTBOOK-ALIGNED` work and monitor formatively.

A precise class-level percentage threshold is intentionally **not frozen before first administration**. The first diagnostic should reveal whether a simple threshold is appropriate for this class.
