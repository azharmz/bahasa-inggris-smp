# Authoritative Textbook Audit

**Title:** *English for Nusantara untuk SMP/MTs Kelas VIII*

**First printing:** 2022  
**ISBN (jil. 2):** 978-602-427-941-7  
**Extent:** xxiv + 312 numbered content pages (PDF 336 pages including front matter)

The uploaded official textbook is the authoritative textbook evidence used for this audit.

## What the Book Says About Its Role

The book's foreword states that the textbook may serve as a reference/inspiration and may be modified in designing learning according to student characteristics, potential, and needs. This supports the project's rule that textbook sequence is not automatically the classroom pacing authority.

## Stated Approach

The preface describes a Genre-Based Approach combined with language-acquisition progression from oral toward written language. Skills are integrated across listening, speaking, reading, viewing, writing, and representing.

The book identifies Grade VIII text types as recount, narrative, descriptive, and procedure. Recount and narrative are described as new in Grade VIII; descriptive and procedure are treated as previously encountered in Grade VII.

**Governance implication:** “previously taught” is a textbook assumption, not evidence that this class has mastered the prerequisite.

## Chapter 0 — The Beginning

Chapter 0 is a broad support/review bank, not a coherent prerequisite course that must be completed linearly.

Actual classroom-language content includes teacher/student expressions for:

- pronunciation (`Would you pronounce...`);
- spelling (`How do you spell that?`);
- asking how to say something in English/Indonesian;
- pair work and page/activity instructions;
- listening/reading participation;
- checking whether students follow;
- time/task/homework instructions.

**Instructional interpretation:** select Chapter 0 items on demand as access language. Do not require mastery of every Chapter 0 grammar/expression before Chapter 1.

## Chapter 1 — Celebrating Independence Day

### Unit 1 — The Champion of Panjat Pinang

Target: talk about personal experiences in the past.

Actual language-focus evidence explicitly explains recounting as talking about past experiences and introduces Past Simple regular (`-d/-ed/-ied`) and irregular forms (examples include `win→won`, `make→made`, `hold→held`).

Task demand progresses from vocabulary/context and listening toward asking/giving information about Independence Day experiences.

**Implicit prerequisites:** common action vocabulary; basic subject–verb meaning; time/past concept; enough sentence comprehension to notice verb-form change.

### Unit 2 — Going to a Parade

Target: identify specific information in personal-experience recounts; past tense plus time connectives; listening/reading for detail.

**Implicit prerequisites:** Unit 1 past-event comprehension plus ability to locate explicit information and follow event sequence.

### Unit 3 — Independence Day at SMP Merdeka

The front-matter scope/sequence parsing contains a title/content presentation inconsistency around Unit 3. This remains flagged for page-level verification before freezing its detailed map.

## Chapter 2 — Kindness Begins with Me

Primary competency: narrative/story comprehension, sequencing, character interpretation, retelling, and modeled rewriting.

Actual task evidence confirms students are asked to use past forms of `to be` / `feel` / `become` with adjectives to describe character traits, behavior, and feelings. Unit 1 also includes a mime game where peers ask past-tense questions (`Did you...?`) and the performer answers in positive/negative past forms.

**Complexity implication:** Chapter 2 already assumes more than recognizing simple-past statements; it draws on question formation, polarity, action vocabulary, feelings/trait adjectives, and event sequencing.

## Chapter 3 — Love Our World

Primary competencies include asking/giving opinions, procedure/instruction work, categorizing/responding to situations, and poster/instruction production.

Because procedure is treated by the book as previously introduced in Grade VII, Grade VIII tasks may not rebuild all foundational procedure concepts. Diagnostic must test actual readiness rather than accepting the textbook assumption.

## Chapter 4 — No Littering

Chapter 4 is a clear extension/revisit of recount competency with greater question and connected-output demands.

### Unit 1 — Did It Rain Last Night?

Actual tasks require students to:

- form Yes/No questions using `Did` or past `to be`;
- create up to 10 interview questions about a classmate's past incident;
- interview a classmate;
- retell the classmate's incident to the class, including when/who, feeling, what happened, what happened next, and optionally a comment.

**Difficulty implication:** this is not an entry-level simple-past lesson. It requires question formation + listening for answers + note/idea organization + third-person oral recount.

### Unit 2 — What Happened to the Sea Animals?

Actual language-focus tasks require Wh- questions using `did + subject + base verb` and distinguish/use `was/were` where appropriate. Reading/viewing then asks for information from environmental incident texts.

### Unit 3 — You Can Help

Actual writing scaffold explicitly develops recount structure through orientation (who/when/where/why/how), chronological records of events, and a final comment/evaluation or emotional response.

The `Your Turn` task also asks students to share an environmental movement/action through social media, invite questions/discussion, and share discussion results. This is a relatively high multimodal/social-production demand and can require adaptation to classroom feasibility.

The chapter reflection summarizes expected outcomes as talking about past incidents/events, making interview questions, identifying main idea/detail, and writing a series of past events.

## Chapter 5 — Embrace Yourself

### Unit 1 — Be Yourself

Actual language focus includes opinion frames such as `I think...`, `I believe...`, `In my opinion...`, and related expressions. Students then discuss opinions about familiar activities/topics.

The unit also requires group performance/retelling from a script: students assign narrator/character roles, consider actions/feelings, practice, then perform the story to the class.

**Difficulty implication:** the unit combines formulaic opinion language with narrative comprehension/performance; these should be separable for adaptation if students are not ready for the full integrated task.

### Unit 2 — I Know I Can Do It

Students identify/order main story events and study adverbs of manner (`-ly` and irregular/non-`-ly` examples such as `well`, `fast`, `hard`).

**Prerequisite interpretation:** adverbs of manner refine how an action is described; they should not block basic narrative/event comprehension.

### Unit 3 — Practice Makes Perfect

Target: write main events / write a similar story from a model. This sits higher on the supported→independent production ladder.

## Cross-Book Task Architecture

The book repeatedly uses a progression compatible with Genre-Based Approach:

```text
activate topic / Say What You Know
    ↓
input through listening / reading / viewing
    ↓
notice vocabulary / language features
    ↓
controlled or collaborative practice
    ↓
Your Turn / performance / writing / representing
    ↓
enrichment + reflection
```

This is useful for lesson design, but the original task difficulty can be reduced while preserving the same progression.

## High-Risk Assumptions for This Class

Do not assume the following simply because the book uses them:

- Grade VII procedure/descriptive prerequisites are stable;
- students can already distinguish `be` sentences from lexical-verb sentences;
- students can form Yes/No and Wh questions;
- students can use enough common past verbs to sustain a recount;
- students can extract detail from a multi-sentence oral text;
- students can connect events into a short oral/written text;
- students are ready for full social-media/performance outputs exactly as printed.

These become diagnostic/adaptation questions.

## Relationship to Current Phase D CP

The textbook broadly supports the three official Phase D element groups:

- Listening–Speaking through oral comprehension, dialogue, interview, retelling, and presentation;
- Reading–Viewing through explicit/detail/main-idea/sequence work across written and multimodal texts;
- Writing–Presenting through guided writing, posters, recount/story production, and performance/representation.

However, the official CP is an end-of-phase destination. Individual textbook tasks must not be treated as mandatory mastery checkpoints independent of student readiness.
