# Grade VIII Prerequisite Map

## Status

`DRAFT v0.1 — TO BE TESTED BY DIAGNOSTIC`

Purpose: identify the smallest enabling competencies students need before higher-complexity Grade VIII tasks become realistic.

This is a **dependency map**, not a fixed linear syllabus. Several branches can develop in parallel.

## 1. Core Access Layer

```text
Classroom task language
    ├─ understand frequent instructions
    ├─ ask for repetition / clarification
    └─ participate in pair/group routines

High-frequency familiar vocabulary
    ├─ people / school / home / activities
    ├─ common actions
    ├─ time words
    └─ basic conditions / feelings
```

These capabilities reduce cognitive load across every later task.

## 2. Sentence Meaning Layer

```text
subject/reference awareness
        ↓
who/what a sentence is about
        ↓
basic predicate meaning
    ├─ BE + noun/adjective/place
    └─ lexical VERB + complement
        ↓
positive sentence meaning
        ↓
negative / question contrast
```

Operationally, students should increasingly recognize that English sentences can express a state/identity/location through `be` or an action through a lexical verb. Terminology itself is secondary to usable understanding.

## 3. Question Layer

```text
understand question intent
        ↓
Yes/No question-response
        ↓
Wh words: who / what / when / where / why / how
        ↓
question structure appropriate to target
    ├─ be → is/are/was/were ...?
    └─ lexical verb → do/does/did + base verb ...?
```

This branch is prerequisite for interviews, information-gap tasks, and Chapter 4 questioning.

## 4. Past Experience / Recount Branch

```text
basic sentence meaning
        +
time reference (yesterday, last..., etc.)
        +
common action verbs
        ↓
past state: was / were
        ↓
regular past verbs + selected high-frequency irregular verbs
        ↓
understand short past-event sentences
        ↓
ask/answer about past events
        ↓
sequence events chronologically
        ↓
short oral recount
        ↓
short written recount
        ↓
orientation → events → optional comment/evaluation
```

**Key design implication:** asking students to recount an experience is not merely a `simple past` grammar task. It depends simultaneously on vocabulary, sentence meaning, time reference, event sequencing, comprehension, and production.

## 5. Narrative Branch

```text
past-event comprehension
        +
people/character vocabulary
        +
feelings/traits adjectives
        ↓
identify character + setting + action
        ↓
identify chronological main events
        ↓
understand character feeling/behavior
        ↓
retell from model/script
        ↓
retell with reduced support
        ↓
write/rewrite modeled story
```

Adverbs of manner are an enrichment/precision layer after students can already understand action + event sequence; they are not a prerequisite for basic story comprehension.

## 6. Procedure / Instruction Branch

```text
common action verbs
        +
follow classroom instructions
        +
sequence concepts (first/next/then/finally)
        ↓
understand short procedural steps
        ↓
identify purpose + ordered actions
        ↓
give short oral instructions
        ↓
write/represent practical instructions
```

Because the textbook assumes procedure has been encountered in Grade VII, diagnostic evidence must determine whether that prerequisite is actually available; grade-level assumption is not evidence of mastery.

## 7. Opinion Branch

```text
familiar topic vocabulary
        +
basic sentence production
        ↓
understand opinion vs factual statement at a simple level
        ↓
formulaic opinion: I think / I believe / In my opinion
        ↓
ask for / respond to opinion
        ↓
opinion + simple reason
        ↓
connected opinion / support
        ↓
Phase-D direction: defend an argument
```

The final node is a phase-end direction and should not be forced prematurely if the lower nodes are unstable.

## 8. Reading / Listening Comprehension Ladder

```text
recognize familiar vocabulary
        ↓
understand short sentence meaning
        ↓
locate explicit detail
        ↓
identify topic/main idea
        ↓
follow sequence / overall flow
        ↓
combine details across sentences
        ↓
infer simple implicit information
```

Support (images, word box, repetition, slower delivery, bilingual clarification) may be used while the underlying competency develops.

## 9. Writing / Presenting Ladder

```text
word / phrase
    ↓
modeled simple sentence
    ↓
controlled sentence variation
    ↓
2–3 connected sentences
    ↓
short guided text
    ↓
short independent text
    ↓
multimodal/presented output appropriate to purpose
```

A student does not need perfect grammar at one layer before attempting the next; the ladder represents increasing independence and discourse complexity.

## 10. Diagnostic Gates

Before freezing Semester 1 Scope & Sequence, diagnostic evidence should answer at minimum:

| Gate | What must be observed |
|---|---|
| G0 Classroom access | Can students follow/seek clarification for basic classroom instructions? |
| G1 Vocabulary access | Can students understand/use common familiar nouns, actions, time words, conditions/feelings? |
| G2 Sentence access | Can students understand/produce very simple `be` and lexical-verb sentences? |
| G3 Polarity/question access | Can students distinguish/produce basic positive, negative, and question forms with support? |
| G4 Information extraction | Can students locate explicit information in a very short familiar oral/written text? |
| G5 Past-event readiness | Can students recognize/use basic past-time reference and some common past forms? |
| G6 Connected output | Can students connect 2–3 ideas/events orally or in writing with support? |

These gates are intentionally compact. The diagnostic should sample them rather than becoming a large exam.

## 11. Current Boundary

This map is ready to guide diagnostic design, but it is **not validated against this class yet**. Any assumption about which gate students pass must remain `UNKNOWN` until classroom evidence is recorded.
